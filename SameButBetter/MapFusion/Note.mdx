## Specification

Let $f : \alpha \to \beta$ and $g : \beta \to \gamma$ be pure functions.
Write $\varepsilon$ for the empty list, $a::x$ for prepending $a$ to $x$,
and $|x|$ for the length of $x$.

\begin{definition}[Output specification]

For finite lists $x=[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\in\operatorname{List}(\alpha)$ and
$y\in\operatorname{List}(\gamma)$, the **specification predicate**
$\mathcal{S}_{f,g}(x,y)$ means that $y$ preserves the length and order of $x$
and replaces each element $x_i$ with $g(f(x_i))$:

$$
\mathcal{S}_{f,g}(x,y)
\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}
\Bigl(|y| = |x| \;\land\; \forall i \in \{1,\ldots,n\},\; y_i = g(f(x_i))\Bigr).
$$

In Lean, `Specification f g xs ys` expresses this relation inductively:
`nil` relates the two empty lists, and `cons` relates their corresponding heads
and tails. Here `xs` and `ys` denote $x$ and $y$.

```lean4 file="./Verification/Specification.lean" declaration="Specification"
```

\end{definition}

## Example

Take $\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\mathbb{N}$, $f(t)=t+1$, $g(t)=2t$, and
$x=[1,2,3]$. The required output is $y=[4,6,8]$. The two implementations
compute it as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Version A:}\quad &[1,2,3]
  \xrightarrow{\operatorname{map}(f)}[2,3,4]
  \xrightarrow{\operatorname{map}(g)}[4,6,8],\\
\text{Version B:}\quad &[1,2,3]
  \xrightarrow{\operatorname{map}(g\circ f)}[4,6,8].
\end{aligned}
$$

## Implementations and correctness

Version A uses two maps; Version B composes the functions within one map:

$$
A_{f,g}(x) = \operatorname{map}(g,\operatorname{map}(f,x)),
\qquad
B_{f,g}(x) = \operatorname{map}(g\circ f,x).
$$

\begin{theorem}[Correctness and equivalence]

For every $x\in\operatorname{List}(\alpha)$, both implementations satisfy the
specification and produce the same output:

$$
\mathcal{S}_{f,g}(x,A_{f,g}(x))
\;\land\;
\mathcal{S}_{f,g}(x,B_{f,g}(x)),
\qquad A_{f,g}(x)=B_{f,g}(x).
$$

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

**Satisfaction.** Induction on $x$ proves `versionA_correct` and
`versionB_correct`. The empty case uses `Specification.nil`; the nonempty case
uses `Specification.cons` and the induction hypothesis.

## Traversal cost

\begin{definition}[Traversal cost]

Assign one unit of cost to each nonempty list cell visited by a map:

$$
C_{\mathrm{map}}(\varepsilon)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}0,\qquad
C_{\mathrm{map}}(a::x)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}1+C_{\mathrm{map}}(x).
$$

`countedMap f xs` returns a pair $(\text{output},\text{cost})$.
Its second component implements this recurrence: zero for `[]`, and
`cost + 1` for `x :: xs`.

```lean4 file="./Verification/Performance.lean" declaration="countedMap"
```

The counted executions `versionACounted` and `versionBCounted` follow the two
implementations. A adds the costs of its two maps; B counts one composed map.

Write $\pi_2$ for the second component of a pair (`.2` in Lean). The costs below
refer precisely to these executions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_A(x)&\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\pi_2(\mathtt{versionACounted}\;f\;g\;x),\\
C_B(x)&\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\pi_2(\mathtt{versionBCounted}\;f\;g\;x).
\end{aligned}
$$

\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[One map]

A counted map returns the ordinary map result and visits exactly $|x|$ cells:

$$
\mathtt{countedMap}\;f\;x=(\operatorname{map}(f,x),|x|).
$$

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $x$: the empty case has cost zero; the nonempty case adds one visit.
This is `countedMap_spec`:

\begin{theorem}[Exact traversal costs]

The counted executions preserve the original outputs and record their costs:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathtt{versionACounted}\;f\;g\;x&=(A_{f,g}(x),2|x|),\\
\mathtt{versionBCounted}\;f\;g\;x&=(B_{f,g}(x),|x|).
\end{aligned}
$$

In particular, $C_A(x)=2|x|$ and $C_B(x)=|x|$.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply `countedMap_spec` twice for A and once for B. Mapping preserves list length.
The two conjuncts of `implementation_costs` are exactly the two equalities above:

For $n=|x|$, these counts imply $C_A(x)-C_B(x)=n$ and $C_B(x)\le C_A(x)$,
with strict inequality exactly when $n>0$. Both have $\Theta(n)$ traversal cost.
The model excludes function evaluation, allocation, and compiler optimizations;
it counts list-cell visits, not measured runtime or memory usage.
